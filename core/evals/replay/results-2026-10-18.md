# Replay results, 2026-10-18

Three shipped tickets from the human's work repo, stages 0-3, Opus,
autonomy L3, no human. Graded by `rubric.md`. Artifacts are kept outside
this repo.

| Ticket | Requirements | Invariants | Key decisions | Defects | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bug: a failed synced write is shown as saved | 3/3 | 3/3 | 10/10 | 0 | better |
| feature: refetch a day whose source changed | 3/3 | 3/3 | 10/10 | +1 | better |
| architecture: one program per model | 2/3 | 2/3 | 8/10 | -2 | mixed |

## What Enjinia did well

- No interview question was refused as a design question (0 of 12).
- In each ticket the concept stage's model checking found a real defect
  the shipped design did not address or had to fix later: a stale rollback
  row within one push; a decision rule that a later fix had to change, and
  a listing/download race; a narrow staleness race present in the shipped
  code.
- It recovered facts the tickets got wrong or left out (a second gate the
  ticket did not name; a throw that sends no reply at all).

## Where it fell short

1. A key decision rested on an unverified hypothesis about a dependency
   whose source it could not read; the decision was wrong and cost both
   defect points in the architecture ticket. Labelling it a hypothesis was
   not enough.
2. With no human, the lead overrode "stop when a decision changes pinned
   behavior" (17 tests) and recorded it in an ADR. The rule says every
   level; the runbook had no behavior for runs without a human.
3. The as-is skipped a file in the blast radius and the design marked that
   component "unchanged", which would have failed at the first write.
4. Two of the architecture ticket's "same" decisions followed from an
   oracle answer that hinted at the design.
5. One ticket took on a connector the human shipped separately; justified
   by the oracle's answer, but larger than the shipped scope.

## Changes made to Enjinia from these results

See the commit that adds this file: a decision resting on an unverified
hypothesis is a gate; runs without a human turn every stop into a recorded
open gate; a component marked unchanged must have been read.
